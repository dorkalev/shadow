# P5.1 — Data Subject Access to Information

## Criterion (AICPA TSP Section 100, verbatim)
> The entity grants identified and authenticated data subjects the ability to access their stored personal information for review and, upon request, provides physical or electronic copies of that information to data subjects to meet the entity's objectives related to privacy. If access is denied, data subjects are informed of the denial and reason for such denial, as required, to meet the entity's objectives related to privacy.

## What it means
The Privacy category is optional — it applies only if the company includes Privacy in its SOC 2 scope (most tiny startups don't; they cover privacy via GDPR/CCPA programs instead).

People have the right to see what personal information you hold about them and to get a copy (this is the DSAR — data subject access request — of GDPR Art. 15 / CCPA fame). Two safeguards are embedded: you must verify the requester's identity before handing over data (otherwise the access mechanism becomes a data-leak vector), and if you deny a request — can't verify identity, legal exemption, manifestly excessive — you must tell the person you denied it and why.

For a small startup this doesn't require a fancy privacy portal. A settings page that shows profile data plus an export button, or a documented manual procedure ("verify via signed-in email, run the export script, send via secure link within 30 days"), fully satisfies it — as long as requests are tracked, deadlines are met, and denials are written down.

## Points of focus (2022 revision, summarized)
These are summaries of the AICPA points of focus, not verbatim:
- The identity of data subjects making access requests is authenticated before access is granted.
- Data subjects can review their personal information and obtain physical or electronic copies upon request.
- Access or copies are provided in understandable form, within a reasonable/committed time, at reasonable or no cost.
- When access is denied, the data subject is informed of the denial and the reason, and of any escalation/appeal avenue, as required.

## What the auditor will ask for
- The documented DSAR/access-request procedure, including identity verification steps and response SLA.
- The intake channel evidence (privacy@ address, in-app export feature, request form).
- The DSAR log/tracker for the period: all requests, dates received, verification, dates fulfilled or denied.
- A completed sample: request through fulfillment (export artifact, delivery method) within SLA.
- Any denial during the period with the written reason communicated to the requester (or attestation of none).
- Evidence the export is complete relative to the data inventory (what stores feed the export).

## How a tiny AI-first startup satisfies it
- Self-serve first: settings page shows profile data; an "Export my data" button generates a JSON/ZIP from the primary stores (account, content/prompts, billing metadata) and emails a time-limited signed URL.
- Manual channel: privacy@yourco.com forwards into Linear (label `dsar-access`, 30-day due date). Identity verification = request must come from, or be confirmed via, the account's registered email; document edge cases in the runbook.
- Runbook `compliance/runbooks/dsar.md`: verification steps, export script invocation, delivery (signed URL, not email attachment), denial template with reason and escalation contact.
- The export script reads the same store list as `compliance/data-inventory.md`, so exports stay complete as data grows.
- Track everything in Linear — the ticket history is your DSAR log.

- Linear `dsar-access` ticket export for the period — the DSAR log.
