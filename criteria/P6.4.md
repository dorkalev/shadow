# P6.4 — Vendor Privacy Commitments and Assessment

## Criterion (AICPA TSP Section 100, verbatim)
> The entity obtains privacy commitments from vendors and other third parties who have access to personal information to meet the entity's objectives related to privacy. The entity assesses those parties' compliance on a periodic and as-needed basis and takes corrective action, if necessary.

## What it means
The Privacy category is optional — it applies only if the company includes Privacy in its SOC 2 scope (most tiny startups don't; they cover privacy via GDPR/CCPA programs instead).

If Privacy is in scope, every vendor that touches personal information — cloud provider, LLM API, email service, analytics, support desk — must give you contractual privacy commitments, typically a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) or equivalent terms limiting use of the data to providing the service. For a tiny startup this is almost always "accept the vendor's standard DPA" rather than negotiating.

The second half is ongoing: you must periodically check that vendors still honor those commitments (review their SOC 2 report, trust page, or subprocessor changes) and act when something goes wrong — e.g., replace a vendor that removed its DPA or had a breach. An annual lightweight vendor review is the standard mechanism.

## Points of focus (2022 revision, summarized)
Summarized from the AICPA 2022 points of focus:
- Privacy commitments (e.g., DPAs, contract clauses) are obtained before granting third parties access to PI.
- Commitments address use limitation, protection, retention/disposal, and subprocessing of PI.
- Vendor compliance is assessed periodically (e.g., annual review of SOC 2 reports, certifications, trust pages) and when triggered by events.
- Identified issues result in corrective action (remediation, contract change, or vendor replacement).
- The vendor population with PI access is identified and kept current.

## What the auditor will ask for
- Vendor register identifying all third parties with access to PI.
- Executed DPAs (or links to accepted online DPAs/terms) for each PI vendor.
- The most recent periodic vendor review: SOC 2 reports/ISO certs/trust-page evidence collected per vendor.
- Vendor risk/review procedure in the vendor management policy.
- Evidence of corrective action for any vendor issue during the period (or attestation none occurred).
- Onboarding records for vendors added during the period showing DPA obtained before PI access.

## How a tiny AI-first startup satisfies it
- Vendor register (`compliance/vendors.md` or sqlite) with columns: vendor, purpose, PI access (Y/N), DPA link/date, SOC 2/ISO status, last_reviewed, next_review.
- Store DPA PDFs or URLs in `compliance/dpas/`; for click-through DPAs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, AWS, Stripe), record the URL and acceptance date.
- Annual review as a recurring Linear ticket: for each PI vendor, refresh their trust-page/SOC 2 status, update `last_reviewed`, note any subprocessor changes. Takes an afternoon at 10-vendor scale.
- New-vendor Linear template requires "DPA obtained" before the ticket closes and before production credentials are issued.
- Corrective action = a Linear ticket: e.g., "Vendor X breach — assess exposure, rotate keys, decide replacement," linked from the register row.

## Evidence artifacts
- `compliance/vendors.md` (or sqlite) vendor register with DPA and review columns.
- `compliance/dpas/` folder: DPA PDFs or a `dpa-links.md` with acceptance dates.
- Annual vendor review Linear ticket with per-vendor notes and completion date.
- Vendor onboarding tickets for the period showing DPA-before-access.
- Corrective-action tickets (if any) linked from register rows.
