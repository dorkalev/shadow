# P2.1 — Choice and Consent Management

## Criterion (AICPA TSP Section 100, verbatim)
> The entity communicates choices available regarding the collection, use, retention, disclosure, and disposal of personal information to the data subjects and the consequences, if any, of each choice. Explicit consent for the collection, use, retention, disclosure, and disposal of personal information is obtained from data subjects or other authorized persons, if required. Such consent is obtained only for the intended purpose of the information to meet the entity's objectives related to privacy. The entity's basis for determining implicit consent for the collection, use, retention, disclosure, and disposal of personal information is documented.

## What it means
The Privacy category is optional — it applies only if the company includes Privacy in its SOC 2 scope (most tiny startups don't; they cover privacy via GDPR/CCPA programs instead).

This criterion is about giving people real choices and honoring them. Where a data subject has options — marketing emails yes/no, analytics cookies yes/no, "may we use your data to improve our models" yes/no — you must tell them the options exist, tell them what each choice costs them (e.g., "without cookies, sessions won't persist"), and capture their decision. Where explicit consent is legally required (sensitive data, GDPR consent basis, model-training use of customer content), you must obtain it before doing the thing, and only for the purpose you stated.

The last sentence matters for startups: most day-to-day processing runs on implicit consent (you signed up, so we process your email to run your account). That's fine — but the basis must be written down. A short document mapping each processing activity to its consent basis (explicit consent, contract, legitimate interest) satisfies this.

## Points of focus (2022 revision, summarized)
These are summaries of the AICPA points of focus, not verbatim:
- Data subjects are informed of the choices available to them and the consequences of each choice, including denying or withdrawing consent.
- Explicit consent is obtained where required (e.g., sensitive personal information, new uses) and is captured and retained.
- Consent is obtained only for the intended, communicated purpose; new purposes require new consent.
- The basis for relying on implicit consent is documented and reviewed.
- Consent preferences can be changed by data subjects, and changes are honored (e.g., unsubscribe, opt-out).

## What the auditor will ask for
- Screenshots or a walkthrough of consent capture at signup (terms/privacy checkbox, marketing opt-in, cookie banner).
- The documented consent basis matrix (processing activity → legal/consent basis).
- Sample consent records from the system (user ID, timestamp, consent version, choice made).
- Evidence that opt-outs are honored: unsubscribe flow, cookie-preference persistence, a sample opted-out user excluded from marketing sends.
- Evidence that any new use of personal information during the period (e.g., model training) triggered a new consent or notice.
- Policy/procedure describing how consent is obtained, stored, and withdrawn.

## How a tiny AI-first startup satisfies it
- Signup flow: unticked-by-default checkbox for marketing; explicit "I agree to the Terms and Privacy Policy" acknowledgment; store `consented_at`, `policy_version`, and each opt-in flag on the user record.
- Cookie banner (where applicable — EU/UK traffic or non-essential cookies): accept/reject with equal prominence; gate analytics scripts on the choice.
- Model-training use of customer content: default OFF, explicit toggle in settings, and a line in the DPA/terms; never train on data from users who haven't opted in.
- Maintain `compliance/consent-basis.md`: a table of every processing activity with its basis (contract, consent, legitimate interest) — this is the "documented basis for implicit consent."
- Withdrawal paths: unsubscribe link in every email (transactional excluded), settings toggle for training/analytics, and a DSAR email address; track any manual withdrawal requests as Linear tickets.
