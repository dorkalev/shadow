# P6.5 — Vendor Breach Notification Commitments

## Criterion (AICPA TSP Section 100, verbatim)
> The entity obtains commitments from vendors and other third parties with access to personal information to notify the entity in the event of actual or suspected unauthorized disclosures of personal information. Such notifications are reported to appropriate personnel and acted on in accordance with established incident-response procedures to meet the entity's objectives related to privacy.

## What it means
The Privacy category is optional — it applies only if the company includes Privacy in its SOC 2 scope (most tiny startups don't; they cover privacy via GDPR/CCPA programs instead).

If Privacy is in scope, this criterion has two halves. First: your contracts with PI-handling vendors must obligate them to tell you when they have (or suspect) a breach involving your data. Standard DPAs — including those from AWS, GCP, OpenAI, Anthropic, Stripe — contain a breach-notification clause, so for a tiny startup this mostly means verifying the clause exists and knowing where vendor notifications will arrive.

Second: when a vendor notification lands, it must reach the right person and be handled through your normal incident-response process — logged as an incident (P6.3), assessed for downstream data-subject/regulator notification (P6.6), and tracked to resolution. The common failure mode is a vendor breach email sitting unread in a personal inbox.

## Points of focus (2022 revision, summarized)
Summarized from the AICPA 2022 points of focus:
- Contracts/DPAs with third parties having PI access include commitments to notify the entity of actual or suspected unauthorized disclosures.
- A channel exists for receiving vendor notifications, and they are routed to responsible personnel.
- Vendor-reported incidents are handled under the entity's incident-response procedures.
- Downstream obligations (notifying data subjects, regulators, customers) triggered by vendor incidents are evaluated.
- Vendor notification handling is documented and retained.

## What the auditor will ask for
- DPAs for a sample of PI vendors, highlighting the breach-notification clause and timeframe.
- The vendor register showing where each vendor's security notices are delivered (contact email registered with vendor).
- Incident response plan section covering vendor-reported incidents.
- Any vendor breach notifications received during the period, traced to incident tickets and resolution.
- Evidence the intake channel (security@ / vendor status subscriptions) is monitored.
- The incident register showing vendor-originated entries (or none, with the process still demonstrable).

## How a tiny AI-first startup satisfies it
- In the vendor register, add columns: `breach_notice_clause` (link to DPA section) and `notice_contact` (which email you registered with the vendor — use security@yourco.com, a Google Group delivering to all founders, never a personal inbox).
- Subscribe security@ to each critical vendor's security-advisory/status list; note the subscription in the register.
- IR plan (`compliance/policies/incident-response.md`) includes a "vendor-reported incident" trigger: open a Linear incident within 24h of a vendor notice, flag PI involvement, evaluate P6.6 notification duties.
- When onboarding a vendor, the Linear template includes "verify breach-notification clause in DPA; register security@ as contact."
- If a vendor notice arrives, handle it exactly like an internal incident: Linear ticket, register row, RCA, notification decision recorded.
