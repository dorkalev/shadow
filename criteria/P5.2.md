# P5.2 — Correction of Personal Information

## Criterion (AICPA TSP Section 100, verbatim)
> The entity corrects, amends, or appends personal information based on information provided by data subjects and communicates such information to third parties, as committed or required, to meet the entity's objectives related to privacy. If a request for correction is denied, data subjects are informed of the denial and reason for such denial to meet the entity's objectives related to privacy.

## What it means
The Privacy category is optional — it applies only if the company includes Privacy in its SOC 2 scope (most tiny startups don't; they cover privacy via GDPR/CCPA programs instead).

If a data subject tells you their information is wrong, you fix it — correct it, amend it, or (where you can't simply overwrite, e.g., historical records) append a note recording their disputed version. Two extra obligations ride along: if you've shared that data with third parties (processors, integrations) and you committed or are required to propagate corrections, you must push the fix downstream; and if you refuse a correction (you believe your record is accurate, or the request can't be verified), you must tell the requester and give the reason.

For most tiny SaaS products this is nearly free: users edit their own profile in settings, which is self-service correction. The residual work is the manual path — corrections users can't make themselves (billing records, data a support agent must change), tracking those requests, syncing corrections to vendors that hold copies (email tool, billing, CRM), and writing down any denial.

## Points of focus (2022 revision, summarized)
These are summaries of the AICPA points of focus, not verbatim:
- Data subjects can request correction, amendment, or appending of their personal information, and requests are acted on.
- Where the entity has committed or is required to do so, corrections are communicated to third parties who received the data.
- If a correction request is denied, the data subject is informed of the denial and the reason, and may record a statement of disagreement where applicable.
- Correction requests and their resolution are documented.

## What the auditor will ask for
- The correction-request procedure (often part of the DSAR runbook) including denial handling.
- Evidence of self-service correction capability (profile settings screenshots) and the manual intake channel.
- The log of manual correction requests for the period with dates and resolutions (or attestation of none).
- A worked sample: correction request → record updated → downstream systems/vendors updated.
- Any denial with the written reason sent to the requester.
- The list of third parties holding PI copies and the mechanism for propagating corrections (sync jobs, manual updates).

## How a tiny AI-first startup satisfies it
- Self-service: name/email editable in settings — this handles the vast majority of corrections with zero process.
- Manual path: privacy@ or support@ requests become Linear tickets (label `dsar-correction`, 30-day due date); verification = same rule as access requests (confirmed via registered account email).
- Downstream sync: keep the vendor-copy map in `compliance/data-inventory.md` (e.g., email in Stripe, email marketing tool, support desk). Where possible, sync automatically (webhook/job updates Stripe customer email when the user changes it); otherwise the runbook lists per-vendor manual steps.
- Append when you can't overwrite: for immutable records (invoices, audit logs), attach a correction note rather than rewriting history.
- Denials: use the template in `compliance/runbooks/dsar.md` — state the reason and offer to record the user's statement of disagreement.
