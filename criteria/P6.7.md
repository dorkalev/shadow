# P6.7 — Accounting of Disclosures on Request

## Criterion (AICPA TSP Section 100, verbatim)
> The entity provides data subjects with an accounting of the personal information held and disclosure of the data subjects' personal information, upon the data subjects' request, to meet the entity's objectives related to privacy.

## What it means
The Privacy category is optional — it applies only if the company includes Privacy in its SOC 2 scope (most tiny startups don't; they cover privacy via GDPR/CCPA programs instead).

If Privacy is in scope, this criterion means that when a data subject asks, you can tell them (a) what personal information you hold about them and (b) to whom it has been disclosed. This is the SOC 2 flavor of a data subject access request (DSAR) / GDPR Article 15 access right, extended to include an accounting of disclosures.

Operationally you need three things: an intake channel for requests (privacy@ or an in-app form), a documented procedure with identity verification and a response deadline (30 days is the common bar, matching GDPR), and the ability to actually produce the answer — a data-inventory query or export script plus your disclosure log (P6.2) filtered to the requester. The auditor tests both the procedure and any requests actually processed during the period.

## Points of focus (2022 revision, summarized)
Summarized from the AICPA 2022 points of focus:
- A process exists for data subjects to request an accounting of PI held and of its disclosures.
- Requesters' identities are verified before information is provided.
- Responses are complete (covering PI held and disclosure recipients) and provided within defined timeframes.
- Requests, responses, and denials (with rationale) are documented and retained.
- Personnel responsible for handling requests are designated.

## What the auditor will ask for
- The DSAR procedure covering access + accounting-of-disclosures requests, with identity verification and SLA.
- The published request channel (privacy policy section, privacy@ address, or in-app form).
- The DSAR log for the period: requests received, verification performed, response dates, outcomes.
- A sample DSAR package: the actual export sent to a requester, showing PI held and disclosure recipients.
- Evidence the response mechanism works (test/demo of the export script or admin tooling).
- Denied or unfulfillable requests with documented rationale.

## How a tiny AI-first startup satisfies it
- Publish privacy@yourco.com in the privacy policy as the DSAR channel; route it to a Google Group all founders receive.
- Procedure (`compliance/policies/dsar-procedure.md`): verify identity (reply from the account email or in-app confirmation), respond within 30 days, record everything.
- Track each DSAR as a Linear ticket from a `dsar` template with checklist: verified → data export generated → disclosure accounting attached → sent → closed. The Linear board *is* the DSAR log.
- Write a small export script (`scripts/dsar-export.py`) that dumps all PI for a user ID across your DB tables, plus the vendor register/disclosure log entries applicable to them — commit it so the capability is demonstrable even with zero requests.
- If zero DSARs occurred, run one self-test per year (export your own founder account) and log it as a ticket.
