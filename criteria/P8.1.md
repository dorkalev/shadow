# P8.1 — Privacy Complaints and Compliance Monitoring

## Criterion (AICPA TSP Section 100, verbatim)
> The entity implements a process for receiving, addressing, resolving, and communicating the resolution of inquiries, complaints, and disputes from data subjects and others and periodically monitors compliance to meet the entity's objectives related to privacy. Corrections and other necessary actions related to identified deficiencies are made or taken in a timely manner.

## What it means
The Privacy category is optional — it applies only if the company includes Privacy in its SOC 2 scope (most tiny startups don't; they cover privacy via GDPR/CCPA programs instead).

If Privacy is in scope, this is the "closed loop" criterion for the whole privacy program. Two obligations: first, a working channel for privacy inquiries, complaints, and disputes — anyone (user, customer, regulator) can raise an issue, it gets tracked, resolved, and the outcome is communicated back to them. Second, you must periodically monitor your own compliance with your privacy commitments (privacy policy, DPAs, the P-series controls) and fix deficiencies promptly.

For a tiny startup this means a privacy@ inbox feeding Linear tickets with an SLA, plus a recurring internal privacy review — a quarterly or annual self-assessment that walks the privacy controls, records findings, and opens remediation tickets. The auditor tests both the complaint trail and the monitoring cadence with remediation follow-through.

## Points of focus (2022 revision, summarized)
Summarized from the AICPA 2022 points of focus:
- A defined process exists for receiving and tracking privacy inquiries, complaints, and disputes.
- Complaints are addressed and resolved, and resolutions are communicated to the complainant within defined timeframes.
- Compliance with privacy objectives, policies, and commitments is periodically assessed (self-assessments, reviews, audits).
- Deficiencies identified through complaints or monitoring are remediated timely, with corrective actions tracked to closure.
- Escalation paths exist for disputes (including regulator involvement) that cannot be resolved directly.

## What the auditor will ask for
- The privacy complaint/inquiry procedure and the published channel (privacy policy contact section).
- The complaint log for the period: each item with receipt date, resolution, communication back, and elapsed time vs. SLA.
- A sample complaint traced end-to-end: intake → investigation → resolution → response to complainant.
- The periodic privacy compliance review: schedule, most recent completed assessment, findings.
- Remediation tickets for deficiencies found, with closure dates demonstrating timeliness.
- Escalation/dispute-handling documentation (e.g., what happens if a complainant is unsatisfied).

## How a tiny AI-first startup satisfies it
- Publish privacy@yourco.com in the privacy policy; deliver it to a Google Group covering all founders so nothing rots in one inbox.
- Every privacy email becomes a Linear ticket labeled `privacy-complaint` (created manually or via an email-to-Linear integration) with an SLA field; the resolution email to the complainant is pasted/linked in the ticket before close. The Linear board is the complaint log — filterable, timestamped, exportable.
- Set an SLA in the procedure (`compliance/policies/privacy-complaints.md`): acknowledge in 5 business days, resolve in 30 days, escalation path named (CEO, then external counsel).
- Quarterly privacy self-assessment as a recurring Linear ticket: walk a short checklist (policies current? disclosure log maintained? DSARs on time? vendor DPAs valid? subprocessor page accurate?), record findings in the ticket, open a remediation ticket per finding.
- Timeliness is proven by ticket timestamps — created/closed dates on complaints and remediation items.
