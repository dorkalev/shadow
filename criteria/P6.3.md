# P6.3 — Record of Unauthorized Disclosures

## Criterion (AICPA TSP Section 100, verbatim)
> The entity creates and retains a complete, accurate, and timely record of detected or reported unauthorized disclosures (including breaches) of personal information to meet the entity's objectives related to privacy.

## What it means
The Privacy category is optional — it applies only if the company includes Privacy in its SOC 2 scope (most tiny startups don't; they cover privacy via GDPR/CCPA programs instead).

If Privacy is in scope, this criterion requires you to keep a register of every unauthorized disclosure of personal information — actual breaches, suspected breaches, misdirected emails, a Slack message pasting customer data into a public channel, an LLM prompt that leaked PI to a vendor without a DPA, or a report from a customer or researcher. The record must capture what happened, when it was detected, what PI and which subjects were affected, and how it was resolved.

The key auditor concern is completeness and timeliness: incidents get logged when detected, not backfilled. Even if zero incidents occurred, you must demonstrate the register exists and the intake channels (incident process, security@ inbox) feed into it. This log is also the foundation for P6.6 breach notification decisions.

## Points of focus (2022 revision, summarized)
Summarized from the AICPA 2022 points of focus:
- Detected or reported unauthorized disclosures, including breaches, are recorded at the time of detection/report.
- Records include the nature of the incident, PI and data subjects affected, dates, and resolution.
- Both internally detected and externally reported incidents are captured.
- Records are retained and support breach-notification obligations and evaluation.
- The record is reviewed for completeness against incident-response activity.

## What the auditor will ask for
- The unauthorized-disclosure/breach register for the audit period (or attestation that it is empty, plus the register itself).
- Incident response plan showing that privacy incidents are classified and logged.
- A sample of incident tickets traced to register entries (severity, PI affected, resolution, dates).
- Evidence of intake channels: security@/privacy@ inbox, on-call escalation, vendor notification routing.
- Post-incident reviews / RCAs for any privacy incidents.
- Reconciliation of the register against the broader incident log (no privacy incidents missing).

## How a tiny AI-first startup satisfies it
- Add a privacy section to your incident register: `compliance/incident-log.md` (or sqlite) with an `unauthorized_disclosure` flag, plus columns for PI categories, subjects affected, detection date, report source, resolution, notification decision.
- Classify privacy impact in your incident-response template (Linear issue template with a mandatory "PI involved? Y/N" field). Every `PI=Y` incident must land in the register.
- Route external reports through security@ and privacy@ inboxes; the on-call founder converts reports to Linear incidents within 24h.
- Even with zero incidents, keep the register committed to the repo with headers and a "no entries this period" review note — that is auditable evidence.
- After any incident, write a short RCA in the ticket and link it from the register row; the notification decision (P6.6) is recorded in the same row.
