# P6.6 — Breach Notification to Affected Parties

## Criterion (AICPA TSP Section 100, verbatim)
> The entity provides notification of breaches and incidents to affected data subjects, regulators, and others to meet the entity's objectives related to privacy.

## What it means
The Privacy category is optional — it applies only if the company includes Privacy in its SOC 2 scope (most tiny startups don't; they cover privacy via GDPR/CCPA programs instead).

If Privacy is in scope, this criterion requires that when a breach or privacy incident occurs, you actually notify the people who need to know: affected data subjects (users), regulators where the law requires it (e.g., GDPR's 72-hour supervisory-authority rule, US state breach laws), and "others" such as business customers whose end-user data you process — often under contractual notification deadlines in your own DPAs with customers.

For a Type II report, the auditor tests the *capability*, not just occurrences: a documented breach-notification procedure with decision criteria, owners, timelines, and templates must exist even if you never had a breach. If a breach did occur, the auditor traces it from the incident register (P6.3) through the notification decision to the actual notices sent.

## Points of focus (2022 revision, summarized)
Summarized from the AICPA 2022 points of focus:
- A breach-notification process defines when and how affected data subjects, regulators, and other parties are notified.
- Notification obligations under applicable laws, regulations, and contracts are identified and tracked.
- Notifications are made within required timeframes and include required content.
- Responsibility for notification decisions is assigned (with legal counsel involvement as needed).
- Notification decisions and communications are documented and retained, including decisions not to notify.

## What the auditor will ask for
- The breach-notification procedure (usually a section of the incident response plan) with decision criteria, roles, and timelines.
- Notification templates (data subject email, regulator filing, customer notice).
- A mapping of applicable notification obligations (GDPR, CCPA/state laws, customer DPA clauses).
- For any breach in the period: the incident record, notification decision memo, copies of notices sent, and timing evidence.
- Evidence of a tabletop exercise or plan review if no breaches occurred.
- Contact/escalation list including legal counsel or breach coach.

## How a tiny AI-first startup satisfies it
- Add a "Breach Notification" section to `compliance/policies/incident-response.md`: severity criteria for notification, who decides (CEO + counsel), regulator deadlines (GDPR 72h, state laws), customer DPA deadlines (commonly 24–72h), and where the decision is recorded.
- Keep notice templates in `compliance/templates/`: `breach-notice-users.md`, `breach-notice-regulator.md`, `breach-notice-customers.md`.
- Maintain an obligations table (`compliance/notification-obligations.md`): jurisdiction/customer → trigger → deadline → recipient. At tiny scale this is ~10 rows.
- Record every notification decision — including "no notification required, rationale: X" — as a field on the incident register row (P6.3) and in the Linear incident ticket.
- Run one annual 30-minute tabletop (simulate a vendor breach → walk the decision tree) and log it as a completed Linear ticket; that is your Type II evidence when no real breach occurred.
