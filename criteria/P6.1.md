# P6.1 — Consent Before Third-Party Disclosure

## Criterion (AICPA TSP Section 100, verbatim)
> The entity discloses personal information to third parties with the explicit consent of data subjects and such consent is obtained prior to disclosure to meet the entity's objectives related to privacy.

## What it means
The Privacy category is optional — it applies only if the company includes Privacy in its SOC 2 scope (most tiny startups don't; they cover privacy via GDPR/CCPA programs instead).

If Privacy is in scope, this criterion says you may only hand personal information (PI) to a third party when the data subject has explicitly agreed, and that agreement must exist *before* the data leaves your systems. "Third parties" includes analytics vendors, LLM API providers, CRMs, support tools, and payment processors — anyone outside your entity who receives PI.

In practice, consent is usually captured in your privacy notice acceptance, terms of service, or an explicit checkbox for optional sharing. The auditor tests that your disclosures map to a consent basis: every vendor or partner receiving PI should trace back to a documented consent mechanism, and new sharing arrangements should trigger a consent review before go-live. Disclosures without a consent record are exceptions.

## Points of focus (2022 revision, summarized)
Summarized from the AICPA 2022 points of focus:
- Explicit consent for disclosure is obtained from data subjects before PI is disclosed to third parties.
- Records of consent (who, what, when, for which purpose) are retained.
- New or changed disclosure purposes trigger renewed consent or notice before disclosure occurs.
- Disclosures are limited to the purposes for which consent was granted.
- Exceptions (legal requirements, court orders) are identified, documented, and handled per policy.

## What the auditor will ask for
- Privacy notice / privacy policy showing what disclosures are made and the consent mechanism.
- The consent capture flow (signup screenshots, checkbox copy, ToS acceptance logs).
- Consent records for a sample of data subjects, with timestamps preceding disclosure dates.
- The list of third parties receiving PI (vendor register) mapped to consent/notice basis.
- Policy covering consent-before-disclosure and handling of legally compelled disclosures.
- Evidence of consent review when a new data-sharing vendor was onboarded during the period.

## How a tiny AI-first startup satisfies it
- Keep a vendor register (`compliance/vendors.md` or a sqlite table) with a `receives_pi` column and a `consent_basis` column (e.g., "privacy policy §4 accepted at signup").
- Capture ToS/privacy-policy acceptance with a timestamp in the users table; that timestamp is your consent-before-disclosure proof.
- Publish disclosures in the privacy policy and subprocessor page; update both *before* enabling a new vendor — enforce via a PR checklist item.
- Route new-vendor onboarding through a Linear ticket template that requires "consent basis documented" before close.
- For AI providers (OpenAI/Anthropic APIs), disclose them explicitly in the privacy notice and record opt-outs if you offer any.
- Log any compelled disclosure (subpoena etc.) in the disclosure log with legal basis noted.
